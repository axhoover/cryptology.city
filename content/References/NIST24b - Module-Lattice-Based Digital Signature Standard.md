# [NIST24b] Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Standard

**Authors:** National Institute of Standards and Technology | **Venue:** Federal Information Processing Standards Publication (FIPS) 204 | [Source](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.FIPS.204)

## Abstract

Digital signatures are used to detect unauthorized modifications to data and to authenticate the identity of the signatory. In addition, the recipient of signed data can use a digital signature as evidence in demonstrating to a third party that the signature was, in fact, generated by the claimed signatory. This is known as non-repudiation since the signatory cannot easily repudiate the signature at a later time. This standard specifies ML-DSA, a set of algorithms that can be used to generate and verify digital signatures. ML-DSA is believed to be secure, even against adversaries in possession of a large-scale quantum computer.

# Notes

The standard gives an overview of ML-DSA as a Fiat–Shamir-with-aborts scheme derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium (Section 3), the parameter sets ML-DSA-44, ML-DSA-65 and ML-DSA-87 with their key and signature sizes (Section 4, Tables 1 and 2), the external key generation, signing and verification algorithms (Section 5, Algorithms 1–3), their internal counterparts (Section 6, Algorithms 6–8) and the pre-hash variant HashML-DSA (Section 5.4). Appendix D lists the differences from version 3.1 of the CRYSTALS-Dilithium submission, from which ML-DSA is derived.
