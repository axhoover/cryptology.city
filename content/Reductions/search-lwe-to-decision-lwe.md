# Search LWE ⇒ Decision LWE

## Statement

Let $q \le \poly(n)$ be prime and $\chi$ a distribution on $\ZZ_q$. If [[learning-with-errors#search-lwe|search LWE]] with parameters $(n, q, \chi)$ is hard for every polynomial number of samples, then [[learning-with-errors#decision-lwe|decision LWE]] with the same $(n, q, \chi)$ is hard for every polynomial number of samples: a distinguisher between $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{A}\mathbf{s} + \mathbf{e})$ and $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{u})$ on $m$ samples with advantage $\varepsilon \ge 1/\poly(n)$ yields an efficient algorithm that, for every $\mathbf{s} \in \ZZ_q^n$, outputs $\mathbf{s}$ from $\poly(n, m, 1/\varepsilon)$ samples with probability exponentially close to $1$ — [[Reg05 - On Lattices, Learning with Errors, Random Linear Codes, and Cryptography|Reg05]] (arXiv v1, Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2).

## Sketch

Since the advantage averages to $\varepsilon$ over uniform $\mathbf{s}$, at least an $\varepsilon/2$ fraction of secrets have advantage at least $\varepsilon/2$; the self-reduction $(\mathbf{a}, b) \mapsto (\mathbf{a}, b + \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{t}\rangle)$ for uniform $\mathbf{t}$, repeated with fresh $\mathbf{t}$ and with acceptance rates estimated empirically, then gives a test that works for every $\mathbf{s}$. To test the guess $s_i = \ell$, map each sample $(\mathbf{a}, b)$ to $(\mathbf{a} + r\mathbf{1}_i,\; b + r\ell)$ for the $i$-th unit vector $\mathbf{1}_i$ and fresh uniform $r \in \ZZ_q$; the result is $(\mathbf{a}', \langle \mathbf{a}', \mathbf{s}\rangle + e + r(\ell - s_i))$, LWE-distributed if $\ell = s_i$ and uniform otherwise, since $q$ is prime. Running the test on all $nq$ pairs $(i, \ell)$, polynomially many because $q \le \poly(n)$, reads off $\mathbf{s}$.

## Notes

- The converse holds for every modulus $q \ge 8$, given $m \ge 32(n \ln q + \secpar)$ samples and error $D_{\ZZ,\alpha q}$ with $\alpha \le 1/4$, and, with the loss stated there, for error with efficiently recognizable support ([[decision-lwe-to-search-lwe|Decision LWE ⇒ Search LWE]]) — folklore.
- **Smooth moduli.** In the normalization with samples $(\mathbf{a}, \langle \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{s} \rangle + e \bmod 1)$, $\mathbf{a}$ uniform over $(q^{-1}\ZZ/\ZZ)^n$ and $e$ a continuous Gaussian of density proportional to $\exp(-\pi x^2/\alpha^2)$, let $q$ be a power of $2$ and $1/q < \alpha < 1/\omega(\sqrt{\log n})$. Then search LWE with parameter $\alpha$ reduces efficiently to decision LWE with parameter $\alpha' = \alpha \cdot \omega(\log n)$, both with arbitrarily many samples — [[MP12 - Trapdoors for Lattices Simpler Tighter Faster Smaller|MP12]] (Theorem 3.1, in the special case stated by [[BLPRS13 - Classical Hardness of Learning with Errors|BLPRS13]], arXiv v1, Theorem 2.17). The reduction of [[MP12 - Trapdoors for Lattices Simpler Tighter Faster Smaller|MP12]] applies to every smooth modulus, one whose prime factors are all polynomially bounded, and essentially subsumes the earlier ones — [[BLPRS13 - Classical Hardness of Learning with Errors|BLPRS13]] (arXiv v1, §1 and §2.3). For $q = \prod_i p_i^{e_i}$ with distinct primes $p_i$ and error of standard deviation $\sigma$, it runs in time polynomial in $\sum_i (p_i + e_i)$, $m$, $n$ and $1/\varepsilon$, needs advantage $\varepsilon > \Omega(\sum_i \exp(-p_i^{2e_i}\sigma^2/q^2))$, and its search algorithm uses $\tilde{O}(m/\varepsilon^2)$ samples — [[MP12 - Trapdoors for Lattices Simpler Tighter Faster Smaller|MP12]] (credited by [[BTV26 - Sample-Preserving Search-to-Decision Reduction for Noisy Linear Equations over Large Moduli|BTV26]], §1 and Table 1). For $q = p_1 \cdots p_k$ a product of distinct primes, [[Pei09 - Public-Key Cryptosystems from the Worst-Case Shortest Vector Problem|Pei09]] gave an earlier reduction, running in time polynomial in $\sum_i p_i$ with the same increase in samples, and for $q = p^e$ with $p$ prime [[ACPS09 - Fast Cryptographic Primitives and Circular-Secure Encryption Based on Hard Learning Problems|ACPS09]] gave one running in time polynomial in $p$ and $e$ (both credited by [[BTV26 - Sample-Preserving Search-to-Decision Reduction for Noisy Linear Equations over Large Moduli|BTV26]], §1).
- **Every modulus.** Let $\delta \in (0, 1/2)$, $m \ge n \ge 1$, $q' \ge 25$, $q$ the smallest power of $2$ with $q \ge q'$, and $\alpha \ge \sqrt{\ln(2n(1 + 16/\delta)/\pi)}/q$. An algorithm with advantage $\zeta$ for decision LWE at modulus $q'$ with $m - (16n + 4\ln\ln q)$ samples and noise at most $\beta = C\alpha\sqrt{n\log(n/\delta)\log(m/\delta)}$, for a universal constant $C$, gives one with advantage at least $(\zeta - \delta)/4$ for decision LWE at modulus $q$ with parameter $\alpha$ and $m$ samples — [[BLPRS13 - Classical Hardness of Learning with Errors|BLPRS13]] (arXiv v1, Corollary 3.3). With the power-of-two case above, applied when $1/q < \alpha/\omega(\log n) < 1/\omega(\sqrt{\log n})$, hardness of search LWE at modulus $q$ with parameter $\alpha/\omega(\log n)$ thus gives hardness of decision LWE at every modulus $q' \ge 25$ with noise at most $\beta$ — [[BLPRS13 - Classical Hardness of Learning with Errors|BLPRS13]] (arXiv v1, §3).
- **Sample-preserving reductions.** The reduction of [[MM11b - Pseudorandom Knapsacks and the Sample Complexity of LWE Search-to-Decision Reductions|MM11b]] turns a distinguisher on $m$ samples into a search algorithm on the same $m$ samples with the same error distribution, for prime-power moduli, and its running time divided by its success probability is polynomial in $m$, $n$, $1/\varepsilon$ and the noise magnitude rather than in $q$ — [[MM11b - Pseudorandom Knapsacks and the Sample Complexity of LWE Search-to-Decision Reductions|MM11b]] (credited by [[BTV26 - Sample-Preserving Search-to-Decision Reduction for Noisy Linear Equations over Large Moduli|BTV26]], §1 and Table 1). For prime $q$ of any size and error $D_\sigma$ with mass proportional to $\exp(-x^2/2\sigma^2)$, $\sigma < q$, a decision algorithm on $m$ samples with advantage $\varepsilon$ and running time $T$ yields a search algorithm on the same samples that runs in time $\poly(n, m, \log q, 1/\varepsilon', T)$ and succeeds with probability $\tilde{\Omega}(\varepsilon'^5)$, where $\varepsilon' = \sigma\varepsilon^2/(q\sqrt{m})$; this is polynomial whenever $q/\sigma$ and $1/\varepsilon$ are, even for exponential $q$ — [[BTV26 - Sample-Preserving Search-to-Decision Reduction for Noisy Linear Equations over Large Moduli|BTV26]] (Corollary 4.4).
