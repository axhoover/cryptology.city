# Group generator

A _group generator_ $\GrGen$ is an efficient randomized algorithm that, on input $1^\secpar$, outputs a description of a cyclic group $\GG$, its order $p$ and a generator $g$, with each element of $\GG$ encoded by one bit string on which the group operation and inversion are efficiently computable. The [[discrete-logarithm#assumption|DLOG]], [[computational-diffie-hellman#assumption|CDH]] and [[decisional-diffie-hellman#assumption|DDH]] games are stated for such a $\GrGen$.

## Definition

A **group generator** is a $\PPT$ algorithm $\GrGen$ that on input $1^\secpar$ outputs a triple $(\GG, g, p)$ of $\poly(\secpar)$ bits, where $\GG$ describes a cyclic group of order $p$, $p$ is written in binary, and $g$ is the encoding of a generator of $\GG$. The description fixes an injective encoding of the elements of $\GG$ as bit strings of $\poly(\secpar)$ bits, and fixed deterministic polynomial-time algorithms, given $\GG$ and the encodings of $a, b \in \GG$, output the encodings of $ab$ and $a^{-1}$. Equality of elements is equality of their encodings.

The triple is short, but the group need not be: $\log p \le \poly(\secpar)$, while $p$ may be superpolynomial in $\secpar$. With $[p] = \{0, \ldots, p - 1\}$, the map $x \mapsto g^x$ is a bijection from $[p]$ to $\GG$, and square-and-multiply computes $g^x$ with $O(\log p)$ group operations, so the challengers of the DLOG, CDH and DDH games are efficient — standard. If DLOG is hard for $\GrGen$, then for every constant $c$ the probability that $p \le \secpar^c$ is negligible: the adversary that tries every $x \in [p]$ when $p \le \secpar^c$ wins with at least that probability — standard.

A [[generic-group-model|generic]] algorithm uses nothing beyond what every group generator provides: the group operation, inversion and equality of encodings. The base notion requires nothing more, and the variants of the assumptions add the rest:

- **Prime order.** [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-prime-order-groups|DLOG in prime-order groups]] and [[decisional-diffie-hellman#ddh-in-prime-order-groups|DDH in prime-order groups]] require $p$ to be prime, so that exponents form the field $\ZZ_p$.
- **Compact encodings.** [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-compactly-encoded-prime-order-groups|DLOG in compactly encoded prime-order groups]] requires encodings of fewer than $2 \lfloor \log p \rfloor$ bits.
- **Membership and verifiability.** The base notion gives no way to decide whether a string encodes an element of $\GG$. [[decisional-diffie-hellman#ddh-in-prime-order-groups-with-decidable-membership|DDH with decidable membership]] adds such a test for the groups $\GrGen$ outputs, and [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-verifiable-prime-order-groups|DLOG in verifiable prime-order groups]] adds an algorithm deciding, for an arbitrary $(\GG, g, p, h)$, whether $\GG$ is a group of prime order $p$ generated by $g$ and $h \in \GG \setminus \{1\}$.
- **Representation by integers.** [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-efficiently-represented-groups|DLOG in efficiently represented groups]] adds mutually inverse deterministic polynomial-time bijections between $\GG$ and $[p]$.
- **A fixed family.** [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-the-multiplicative-group-of-a-prime-field|DLOG in the multiplicative group of a prime field]] and [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-prime-order-subgroups-of-twisted-edwards-curves|DLOG in prime-order subgroups of twisted Edwards curves]] fix the kind of group.

A _bilinear group generator_, also written $\GrGen$, outputs groups $\GG_1, \GG_2, \GG_T$ of prime order $p$, generators $g_1$ of $\GG_1$ and $g_2$ of $\GG_2$, and a pairing $e : \GG_1 \times \GG_2 \to \GG_T$; see [[pairings#type-3|Type 3 pairings]] and [$q$-DLOG](discrete-logarithm#q-dlog).

## Standard instances

### The multiplicative group of a prime field

For a prime $P$, the nonzero residues modulo $P$ form a cyclic group $\ZZ_P^*$ of order $P - 1$ under multiplication — [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Theorem 2.18). Elements are encoded as integers in $[1, P - 1]$, multiplied modulo $P$ and inverted by the extended Euclidean algorithm — [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Appendix C.11). Given the prime factors of $P - 1$, an element $g$ generates $\ZZ_P^*$ if and only if $g^{(P-1)/r} \not\equiv 1 \pmod P$ for every prime $r \mid P - 1$ — [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Claim 2.27). The order $P - 1$ is even for $P > 2$, and for a generator $g$ the Legendre symbol of $g^x$ is $(-1)^x$, so it reveals $x \bmod 2$ — standard; see [[decisional-diffie-hellman#attacks|DDH attacks]].

### Prime-order subgroups of the multiplicative group

For primes $p$ and $P$ with $p \mid P - 1$, the cyclic group $\ZZ_P^*$ has exactly one subgroup of order $p$,

$$
\{x \in \ZZ_P^* : x^p \equiv 1 \pmod P\} = \{h^{(P-1)/p} : h \in \ZZ_P^*\}.
$$

It has prime order, so every element other than $1$ generates it, and $x^p \equiv 1 \pmod P$ decides membership — standard. A group generator of this kind outputs $P$, $p$ and $g = h^{(P-1)/p}$ for some $h \in \ZZ_P^*$ with $g \ne 1$, and encodes elements as integers in $[1, P - 1]$; its groups are called _Schnorr groups_.

The **quadratic residues modulo a safe prime** are the case $P = 2p + 1$: the subgroup of order $p$ is the group of squares $\{x^2 \bmod P : x \in \ZZ_P^*\}$, membership in it is Euler's criterion $x^{(P-1)/2} \equiv 1 \pmod P$ ([[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]], Theorem 2.22), and $g = h^2 \bmod P$ generates it for every $h \in [2, P - 2]$ — standard. Its encodings are compact in the sense of [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-compactly-encoded-prime-order-groups|DLOG in compactly encoded prime-order groups]].

### Elliptic curves

Let $\FF_q$ be a finite field of characteristic other than $2$ and $3$, and let $a, b \in \FF_q$ with $4a^3 + 27b^2 \ne 0$. The elliptic curve $E : y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$, in short Weierstrass form, has as its $\FF_q$-points the set $E(\FF_q)$ of solutions $(x, y) \in \FF_q^2$ together with a point $\infty$, the point at infinity; the condition $4a^3 + 27b^2 \ne 0$ makes the curve nonsingular — [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Definition C.36). Written additively, with $k \cdot Q$ the $k$-fold sum $Q + \cdots + Q$, $E(\FF_q)$ is an abelian group with identity $\infty$ under the following law — [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Appendix C.11):

- $-(x, y) = (x, -y)$, and $Q_1 + Q_2 = \infty$ whenever $Q_2 = -Q_1$;
- otherwise, for $Q_1 = (x_1, y_1)$ and $Q_2 = (x_2, y_2)$, $Q_1 + Q_2 = (x_3, y_3)$ with $x_3 = \alpha^2 - x_1 - x_2$ and $y_3 = \alpha(x_1 - x_3) - y_1$, where $\alpha = (y_2 - y_1)/(x_2 - x_1)$ if $x_1 \ne x_2$, and $\alpha = (3x_1^2 + a)/(2y_1)$ if $Q_1 = Q_2$.

For a prime $P > 3$, Hasse's inequality bounds the order: $|\#E(\FF_P) - (P + 1)| \le 2\sqrt{P}$ — [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Appendix C.11.4). Over every finite field, $|\#E(\FF_q) - (q + 1)| \le 2\sqrt{q}$. TODO: cite Hasse's theorem for prime powers $q$. Schoof's algorithm computes $\#E(\FF_q)$ deterministically in time polynomial in $\log q$ — [[Sch85 - Elliptic Curves over Finite Fields and the Computation of Square Roots mod p|Sch85]] (Section 3, as credited by [[Poo17 - Using Zeta Functions to Factor Polynomials over Finite Fields|Poo17]], Section 1); for a prime field, [[GB08 - Lecture Notes on Cryptography|GB08]] (Appendix C.9.10) state its running time as $O(\log^9 P)$.

A group generator of this kind outputs a description of $\FF_q$, the coefficients $a, b$, a prime $p$ and a point $g \ne \infty$ with $p \cdot g = \infty$, and its group is $\langle g \rangle$, of order $p$; the integer $h = \#E(\FF_q)/p$ is the _cofactor_. When $p^2 \nmid \#E(\FF_q)$, $\langle g \rangle$ is the only subgroup of $E(\FF_q)$ of order $p$, so a point $Q \in E(\FF_q)$ lies in it if and only if $p \cdot Q = \infty$, and $h \cdot Q \in \langle g \rangle$ for every $Q \in E(\FF_q)$ — standard. With cofactor $1$, $E(\FF_q)$ itself has prime order and every point other than $\infty$ generates it. Points are encoded by their affine coordinates, with one more string for $\infty$, or compressed to $x$ and one bit that selects $y$ among the two roots; [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-compactly-encoded-prime-order-groups|DLOG in compactly encoded prime-order groups]] says which encodings are compact.

### Twisted Edwards curves

Let $\FF_q$ be a finite field of characteristic other than $2$ and $3$, and let $a, d \in \FF_q$ be distinct and nonzero. The twisted Edwards curve $E_{a,d} : ax^2 + y^2 = 1 + dx^2y^2$ has identity $(0, 1)$, negation $-(x, y) = (-x, y)$, and addition

$$
(x_1, y_1) + (x_2, y_2) = \left(\frac{x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1}{1 + d x_1 x_2 y_1 y_2},\ \frac{y_1 y_2 - a x_1 x_2}{1 - d x_1 x_2 y_1 y_2}\right)
$$

wherever the denominators are nonzero; it is birationally equivalent to a curve in short Weierstrass form — [[MHM08 - Division Polynomials for Twisted Edwards Curves|MHM08]] (Section 3). Twisted Edwards curves generalize Edwards curves and include every elliptic curve in Montgomery form — [[BBJLP08 - Twisted Edwards Curves|BBJLP08]]. A group generator of this kind outputs the curve and a point $g$ of prime order $p$, and its group is $\langle g \rangle$; this is the setting of [[discrete-logarithm#dlog-in-prime-order-subgroups-of-twisted-edwards-curves|DLOG in prime-order subgroups of twisted Edwards curves]] and of [[digital-signature#eddsa|EdDSA]].
